## When to use

Use this when the water supply fails. Clean hands matter most after using the toilet, changing diapers, caring for someone sick, or touching garbage or animals, and before preparing food or eating.

## What you need

- Soap and clean water (safe tap water, or water that is [boiled](article:boiling-water) or [disinfected](article:bleach-water))
- Hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol
- A clean towel
- Wet wipes and disposable gloves

## Steps

1. If you have soap and clean water, use them. They work best.
2. Wet your hands with clean water and put on soap.
3. Lather the backs of your hands, between your fingers and under your nails.
4. Scrub for at least 20 seconds.
5. Rinse with clean water, and dry with a clean towel or let your hands air-dry.
6. If you have no soap and water, use hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol.
7. Put enough on one palm to cover all of both hands.
8. Rub your hands and fingers together until they are dry, about 20 seconds.

## Warnings

- Hand sanitizer does not work well on hands that are visibly dirty or greasy, and does not remove chemicals.
- Sanitizer does not kill all germs, such as norovirus. Use soap and water when you can.
- Watch young children using sanitizer so they don't swallow it.
- Never wash hands with water that may be unsafe.

## Common mistakes

- Wiping or rinsing off sanitizer before it is dry.
- Scrubbing for only a few seconds.

## Get help

Keep hand sanitizer, wet wipes and disposable gloves at home, so you have them when the water stops. See also [Water outage](article:water-outage).
