## When to use

Store drinking water before an emergency that could interrupt or contaminate your tap water. Stored water can be used for drinking, cooking, and brushing teeth.

Plan for everyone in your household. The US CDC recommends at least 4 liters (about 1 gallon) per person each day for three days. Norway's DSB recommends about 20 liters (5.3 gallons) per person for one week. These are different national preparedness recommendations, not equivalent daily amounts.

## What you need

- Safe drinking water from the tap or unopened commercially bottled water
- Food-grade water containers with tight-fitting lids
- Soap and clean water for washing containers
- Unscented liquid chlorine bleach containing 5% to 9% sodium hypochlorite, if sanitizing reusable containers
- A measuring spoon
- Labels and a marker

## Steps

1. Choose food-grade containers with tight-fitting lids for water you will fill yourself.
2. Wash each reusable container with soap and water.
3. Rinse each container completely.
4. Mix 5 mL (1 teaspoon) of suitable unscented bleach into 1 liter (about 1 quart) of water.
5. Pour the sanitizing mixture into the empty container.
6. Close the lid and shake until the mixture touches every inside surface.
7. Wait at least 30 seconds.
8. Empty the sanitizing mixture from the container.
9. Let the empty container air-dry.
10. Fill the sanitized container with safe, cold tap water.
11. Close the lid tightly.
12. Label the container as drinking water and write the storage date.
13. Store the water in a cool place away from sunlight, gasoline, and pesticides.
14. Replace water you filled yourself every six months.
15. Use a clean scoop when removing water, without touching the water or inside surfaces with your hands.

## Warnings

- Never store drinking water in containers previously used for bleach, pesticides, fuel, or other toxic chemicals.
- Use only unscented bleach with 5% to 9% sodium hypochlorite for the sanitizing mixture described here.
- Do not assume that unsafe tap water becomes safe just because it is stored in a clean container.
- Keep water containers away from gasoline, pesticides, and other chemicals.
- Some people need more stored water, including pregnant people, sick people, and people in hot climates. Include pets in your planning.
- The CDC advises replacing home-filled water every six months. DSB says properly stored Norwegian tap water can remain safe for years, but recommends yearly replacement for taste.

## Common mistakes

- Storing too little water for the number of people in the household.
- Filling containers without washing and sanitizing them first.
- Using containers that previously held hazardous chemicals.
- Leaving water containers in direct sunlight or near chemicals.
- Touching stored water with unclean hands or utensils.
- Forgetting to date containers and replace home-filled water.

## Get help

If your water supply is interrupted or authorities warn that tap water is unsafe, follow local water advisories. Use unopened bottled water or another confirmed safe supply for drinking and food preparation. If you do not have safe water, consult local emergency or public health authorities for instructions on obtaining it.
