## When to use

Use this to make water safe to drink when you cannot [boil it](article:boiling-water). Bleach kills most germs, but boiling is better when you can do it.

## What you need

- Plain, unscented household chlorine bleach, less than 1 year old
- A clean dropper or measuring spoon
- A clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter
- Clean containers with tight lids

## Steps

1. Read the bleach label. If it has instructions for disinfecting drinking water, follow them.
2. Check that the active ingredient is sodium hypochlorite, and note its strength in percent.
3. If the water is cloudy, let it settle. Then pour the clearer water through a clean cloth or filter.
4. For bleach with 5–9% sodium hypochlorite, add 2 drops per liter (quart), or 8 drops per 3.8 liters (1 gallon).
5. For 1% bleach, add 10 drops per liter (quart), or 40 drops per 3.8 liters (1 gallon).
6. If the water is cloudy, colored or very cold, use double the amount.
7. Stir the water, then let it stand for at least 30 minutes.
8. Smell the water. It should have a slight smell of chlorine.
9. If it doesn't, add the same dose again and wait another 15 minutes.
10. Store the water in clean containers with tight lids.

## Warnings

- **Never use scented or color-safe bleach, or bleach with added cleaners.**
- Bleach does not remove fuel, toxic chemicals, heavy metals or salt. If water may contain these, do not drink it: find another source.
- Bleach works less well than boiling against some germs, such as Cryptosporidium and Giardia. Boil the water if you can.
- Do not use water that has floating material, a dark color or a strange smell.

## Common mistakes

- Using old bleach. It loses strength after about a year.
- Drinking the water before the 30 minutes are up.
- Using untreated water to brush teeth, cook, make drinks or wash dishes.

## Get help

If you think the water has fuel or chemicals in it, ask your local health department for advice. A flooded private well must be disinfected and tested afterward: contact the health department.
