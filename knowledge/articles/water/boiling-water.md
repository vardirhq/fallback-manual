## When to use

Use this when you have to drink water that may contain germs: from rivers, lakes, streams, rain barrels, or a tap after a boil-water notice or flood.

## What you need

- A pot or kettle and a heat source
- A clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter
- Clean containers with lids

## Steps

1. If the water is cloudy, let it settle, then pour the clearer water through a clean cloth or filter.
2. Bring the water to a rolling boil, with big bubbles that keep coming even when you stir.
3. Keep it at a rolling boil for 1 minute. Above about 2,000 m (6,500 ft) altitude, boil for 3 minutes.
4. Let it cool naturally. Do not add ice unless the ice was made from safe water.
5. Pour it into clean containers and cover them.
6. To improve the flat taste, pour the water back and forth between two clean containers.

## Warnings

- Boiling kills germs but does not remove chemicals, fuel, salt or heavy metals. Do not rely on it for water that may be polluted by chemicals or floodwater from industrial areas.
- If you cannot boil water, see other purification methods (to be added).
- Handle boiling water carefully to avoid scalds. See [Burns and scalds](article:burns).

## Common mistakes

- Stopping at a simmer instead of a rolling boil.
- Storing boiled water in dirty or uncovered containers.
- Using untreated water to brush teeth, wash food or make ice.

## Get help

If people get severe diarrhea, vomiting or fever after drinking water, keep them drinking safe fluids and get medical help, especially for children and older people.
