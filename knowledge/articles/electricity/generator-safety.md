## When to use

Use this when you run a portable generator, for example during a power outage. Generator exhaust contains carbon monoxide, a poison you cannot see or smell. A generator used indoors can kill in minutes.

## What you need

- The generator's owner's manual
- Heavy-duty extension cords
- Battery-powered carbon monoxide alarms

## Steps

1. Read and follow the labels, instructions and warnings on the generator and in its manual.
2. Place the generator outdoors, at least 6 m (20 ft) from the house, windows, doors, vents and attached garages.
3. Point the exhaust away from your home and other buildings.
4. Keep the generator dry and protected from rain and flooding.
5. Connect appliances to the generator with heavy-duty extension cords.
6. Install battery-powered carbon monoxide alarms on every floor and near sleeping areas. Test them monthly.
7. Let the generator cool down before you refuel it.
8. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds or anyone feels ill, get outside at once. Then call the emergency number.

## Warnings

- **Never run a generator inside** a home, garage, basement, crawlspace or shed, or on a porch, even with doors and windows open.
- Do not touch a wet generator or anything connected to it. You can get an electric shock.
- Fuel spilled on hot engine parts can catch fire.
- Carbon monoxide causes headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, sleepiness and confusion. See [Carbon monoxide poisoning](article:carbon-monoxide).

## Common mistakes

- Running the generator in a garage with the door open.
- Placing it near an open window, door or vent.
- Ignoring a carbon monoxide alarm.

## Get help

If you suspect carbon monoxide poisoning, get everyone outside into fresh air, then call the emergency number. During long power outages, check on your neighbors.
