## When to use

Use this when someone has had an electric shock, for example from a faulty appliance, a frayed cable or a live wire. They may have burns, trouble breathing, a weak or uneven pulse, or be unconscious. Some injuries can't be seen.

## What you need

- A dry wooden broom handle or pole
- Something dry to stand on, such as a plastic mat or wooden box

## Steps

1. Look first, and do not touch. They may still be in contact with the current.
2. Switch off the power at the main switch or fuse box, or unplug the appliance.
3. If you can't switch it off, stand on something dry, such as a plastic mat or a wooden box.
4. Push their limb away from the source with a dry wooden broom handle or pole.
5. If that fails, loop a rope around their ankles or under their arms. Pull them away without touching them.
6. Once contact is broken, check whether they respond and are breathing normally.
7. Call the emergency number.
8. If they are not breathing normally, start [CPR](article:cpr).
9. Cool any burns under running water. See [Burns and scalds](article:burns).

## Warnings

- **If a power line is down, stay at least 6 m (20 ft) away.** Never try to move a live power cable yourself.
- Take extra care if the person is in water. Water conducts electricity.
- Call the emergency number if they passed out even for a second, breathe very fast or slowly, have a very fast, slow or uneven heartbeat, or fell.

## Common mistakes

- Touching the person before the power is off.
- Skipping the doctor after a mild shock. Any shock can cause hidden damage.

## Get help

After any electric shock, see a doctor or go to the emergency department as soon as possible, even if the person seems fine. The heart needs to be checked.
