## When to use

Use this when the power goes out at home. Be ready to manage without power for up to a week.

## What you need

- Flashlights or headlamps, and spare batteries
- Warm clothes, blankets or sleeping bags
- Food that keeps without a refrigerator, and drinking water

## Steps

1. Use flashlights or headlamps rather than candles.
2. Keep the refrigerator and freezer closed. See [Keeping food safe without a refrigerator](article:food-safety-power-outage).
3. Unplug appliances and electronics, so power surges can't damage them when power returns.
4. If you rely on a medical device or a cold-stored medicine, start your backup plan now.
5. Stay warm. See [Staying warm when the power is out](article:staying-warm-power-outage).
6. If your home gets too hot or too cold, go to friends, family or a community shelter.
7. Ask local officials which heating or cooling places are open near you.
8. Stay away from fallen power lines.

## Warnings

- **Never heat your home with a gas stove or oven.** It can cause [carbon monoxide poisoning](article:carbon-monoxide).
- Use grills, camp stoves and generators only outdoors. See [Using a generator safely](article:generator-safety).
- If you must use candles, keep them away from anything that burns and never leave them alone.
- Never siphon gasoline with your mouth.
- Throw out refrigerated medicine after a day without power, unless the label says otherwise. If a life depends on it, use it until you get more.

## Common mistakes

- Opening the refrigerator and freezer often.
- Leaving appliances plugged in when the power comes back.

## Get help

If anyone gets a headache, dizziness, weakness or nausea while fuel is burning nearby, get everyone outside and call the emergency number. Ask your doctor or pharmacist for new medicine if yours spoiled.
