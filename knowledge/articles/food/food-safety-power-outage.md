## When to use

Use this when the power is out and your fridge or freezer has stopped, or when you need to store food without refrigeration.

## What you need

- A thermometer for the fridge and freezer, if you have one
- Coolers and ice, if available
- Non-perishable food: canned goods, dried food, crackers, peanut butter

## Steps

1. Keep the fridge and freezer doors closed as much as possible.
2. Remember that a closed fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours.
3. A full, closed freezer keeps food frozen for about 48 hours; a half-full one for about 24 hours.
4. Eat perishable food from the fridge first, then from the freezer, then non-perishable food.
5. If the outage will last longer than 4 hours, move milk, meat, fish and leftovers into a cooler with ice.
6. Group frozen items together in the freezer so they stay cold longer.
7. When power returns, throw out perishable food that has been above 4 °C (40 °F) for more than 2 hours.
8. Frozen food that still has ice crystals or is at 4 °C (40 °F) or below can be refrozen or cooked.

## Warnings

- You cannot tell whether food is safe by smelling or tasting it.
- When in doubt, throw it out.
- Never taste food to check whether it has gone bad.
- Do not store food outside in snow unless it stays reliably cold, out of the sun, and away from animals.

## Common mistakes

- Opening the fridge repeatedly to check on food.
- Keeping cooked leftovers, meat, fish, eggs or dairy that have been warm for hours.
- Forgetting that canned food is safe to eat cold.

## Get help

Seek medical help for food poisoning with high fever, blood in stools, signs of dehydration, or symptoms lasting more than a few days, and sooner for children, older people and pregnant people.
