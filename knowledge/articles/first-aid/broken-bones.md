## When to use

Use this when a fall or blow may have broken a bone. Signs are a lot of pain, swelling, bruising, trouble moving it, a limb that looks bent, twisted or shorter, a grating feeling, or bone sticking out of a wound.

You can't tell a break from a bad sprain without an X-ray. If in doubt, treat it as broken.

## What you need

- Cushions, clothing or blankets for support and padding
- A clean dressing or cloth for any wound
- A towel or scarf to use as a sling for an arm

## Steps

1. Tell them to keep still. Do not ask them to move the injured part.
2. Support the injury by holding the limb above and below it, or with cushions or folded clothing.
3. Pad around it with clothing or blankets. Do not force it straight.
4. Remove rings, watches and anything else around the injured limb right away.
5. Cover any wound with a clean dressing. Press around the wound, but not on the bone.
6. Call the emergency number for a bone sticking out, a broken leg or hip, or a possible back, neck or pelvis injury.
7. For an arm, support it with a sling, such as a folded towel.
8. Keep supporting the injury until they get medical help.

## Warnings

- Do not move the person until the injury is supported, unless they are in danger.
- **Never try to push a dislocated joint back into place.**
- Do not raise a leg that may be broken, or the legs if the hip or pelvis may be hurt.
- Do not let them eat or drink, in case they need surgery.
- Cold, sweaty skin, a fast weak pulse, fast shallow breathing, or pale, gray or blue skin can mean shock. Call the emergency number.
- Numbness, tingling, or a limb that changes color also needs emergency help.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking it is not broken because they can still move it.
- Trying to straighten the limb by force.

## Get help

Every possible break needs medical treatment as soon as possible. Call the emergency number if they can't move or are in a lot of pain. For a smaller injury, someone else should drive them to the hospital: do not drive yourself.
