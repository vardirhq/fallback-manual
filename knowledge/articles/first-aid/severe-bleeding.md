## When to use

Use this when blood is flowing steadily, spurting, pooling on the ground, or soaking through clothing, or when the person is getting pale, cold or confused from blood loss.

## What you need

- Gloves or a plastic bag over your hands, if you have them
- Clean cloth, gauze, a T-shirt or anything absorbent
- For arms and legs, if pressure does not work: a commercial tourniquet, or a strip of strong cloth at least 4 cm (1.5 in) wide and a stick

## Steps

1. Call emergency services if there is any way to do so, or send someone to get help.
2. Protect your hands if you can, but do not delay to find gloves.
3. Find where the blood is coming from. Remove or cut clothing away if needed.
4. Press hard, directly on the wound, with a pad of cloth. Use the heel of your hand and your body weight.
5. Keep pressing without stopping for at least 10 minutes. Do not lift the pad to check.
6. If blood soaks through, put more cloth on top and keep pressing. Do not remove the first pad.
7. Use a tourniquet only if the wound is on an arm or leg, the bleeding is life-threatening, and pressure is not stopping it.
8. Apply the tourniquet 5–7 cm (2–3 in) above the wound, not over a joint.
9. Tighten it until the bleeding stops. Write down the time it was applied.
10. Once bleeding is controlled, bandage the pad firmly in place.
11. Lay the person down, keep them warm, and watch their breathing until help arrives.

## Warnings

- Do not loosen or remove a tourniquet once applied. Leave that to medical staff.
- A tourniquet is painful when it is working. That is expected.
- Do not remove objects stuck in a wound. Press around them instead.
- Thin cord, wire or string can cut into the skin and do not make good tourniquets.

## Common mistakes

- Lifting the pad to look, which restarts the bleeding.
- Pressing too lightly or for too short a time.
- Placing a tourniquet too loosely, which can make bleeding worse.

## Get help

Severe bleeding always needs medical care, even if it stops. Get the person to help as soon as possible, and tell responders if a tourniquet was used and at what time.
