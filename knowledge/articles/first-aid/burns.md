## When to use

Use this for burns from flames, hot surfaces, hot liquids or steam. For chemical burns, rinse with lots of water and get help. For electrical burns, make sure the power is off before touching the person.

## What you need

- Cool, clean running water, or the cleanest cool water you have
- Cling film, a clean plastic bag, or a clean non-fluffy cloth

## Steps

1. Stop the burning: move the person away from the heat, put out flames, and remove them from danger.
2. Cool the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes. Start as soon as possible.
3. While cooling, remove rings, watches and clothing near the burn, unless they are stuck to the skin.
4. Keep the rest of the person warm so they do not get cold while you cool the burn.
5. After cooling, cover the burn loosely with cling film laid on in strips, or a clean plastic bag or non-fluffy cloth.
6. Give pain relief if available and the person can normally take it.

## Warnings

- Do not use ice, iced water, butter, oil, toothpaste or creams.
- Do not burst blisters.
- Do not pull off clothing that is stuck to the burn.
- Cooling large burns on children or older people can make them dangerously cold. Cool the burn, not the whole person.

## Common mistakes

- Cooling for only a minute or two.
- Wrapping cling film tightly around a limb; lay it on loosely, as the area will swell.
- Using fluffy cotton wool, which sticks to the burn.

## Get help

Get medical help for burns that are larger than the person's hand, deep, white or charred, on the face, hands, feet, joints or genitals, caused by chemicals or electricity, or on a child, an older person, or anyone who is pregnant or has breathing problems after smoke.
