## When to use

Use this after a bump, blow or fall to the head. Signs include headache, a lump, dizziness, feeling sick, confusion, blurred vision, and not remembering what happened.

The brain can be shaken inside the skull (concussion). Some signs appear right away, others only after hours or days.

## What you need

- An ice pack or bag of frozen vegetables, and a cloth to wrap it in
- A clean pad or dressing for any wound

## Steps

1. If they are unresponsive, call the emergency number and check their breathing.
2. If they are not breathing normally, start [CPR](article:cpr).
3. If you suspect a serious injury, do not move them unless they are in danger. The neck or back may be hurt.
4. If they are awake, help them sit down and rest.
5. Wrap something cold in a cloth and hold it on the injury for up to 20 minutes.
6. Press on any bleeding wound with a clean pad.
7. Watch them closely for danger signs, listed under Warnings.
8. Call the emergency number at once if a danger sign appears.
9. Make sure an adult stays with them for at least the first 24 hours.

## Warnings

- **Call the emergency number** if they were knocked out and haven't woken, can't stay awake, have a seizure, vomit repeatedly, or seem confused or behave strangely.
- Also call for slurred speech, weakness or numbness, one pupil larger than the other, a headache that gets worse, or clear fluid or blood from the ears or nose.
- Also call after a fall from higher than 1 m (about 3 ft) or 5 stairs, or a high-speed accident such as a car crash.
- Never put ice directly on the skin.
- Do not drive yourself to the hospital after hitting your head.

## Common mistakes

- Letting someone go back to a sport right away. They must not return until a health professional has checked them.
- Leaving them alone in the first 24 hours.
- Thinking they are fine because they feel OK at first.

## Get help

Get medical advice after any head injury if they are over 65, take blood thinners, had a brain injury or brain surgery before, have been drinking alcohol or taking drugs, or have no one to look after them. For a child who won't stop crying or won't eat after a blow to the head, call the emergency number. See a doctor if symptoms last more than 2 weeks.
