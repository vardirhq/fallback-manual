## When to use

Use this when someone has been cold for a while and shows signs such as shivering, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness, clumsiness, or pale, cold skin. In severe hypothermia the shivering may stop, which is a bad sign, not an improvement.

## What you need

- Shelter from wind, rain and snow
- Dry clothes, blankets, sleeping bags, coats
- Something to put between the person and the ground (foam mat, branches, cardboard)
- Warm, sweet drinks if the person is fully awake

## Steps

1. Get the person out of the cold, wind and wet, into shelter if possible.
2. Handle them gently. Rough movement can trigger dangerous heart rhythms in severe cases.
3. Remove wet clothing and replace it with dry layers.
4. Insulate them from the ground and wrap them in blankets, including the head and neck.
5. Warm the center of the body first: chest, neck, head and groin. Skin-to-skin contact under blankets works.
6. If they are fully awake and can swallow, give warm, sweet drinks and some food.
7. Stay with them and keep them lying down and wrapped until they are warm and alert.
8. If they stop breathing normally, [start CPR](article:cpr).

## Warnings

- Do not give alcohol.
- Do not rub or massage their arms and legs.
- Do not put them in a hot bath or use direct heat such as a heater or hot water bottle straight on the skin; it can burn and can harm the heart. Wrap heat sources in cloth.
- Do not give drinks to someone who is drowsy or confused, as they may choke.

## Common mistakes

- Warming the hands and feet first instead of the body's core.
- Leaving the person on cold ground.
- Thinking the person is improving because the shivering has stopped.

## Get help

Hypothermia can be fatal. Get medical help for anyone who was confused, drowsy, stopped shivering, or was in cold water, even if they seem to recover.
