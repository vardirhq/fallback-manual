## When to use

Use this when a child (1 year to puberty) or a baby (under 1 year) does not respond and is not breathing normally. For adults, see [CPR (adults)](article:cpr).

## What you need

- A firm, flat surface
- A defibrillator (AED), if one is nearby

## Steps

1. Tap a child's shoulders, or a baby's foot, and call their name. If there is no response, shout for help.
2. Tilt the head back and lift the chin. Tilt a baby's head only slightly, so the face points straight up.
3. Look, listen and feel for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
4. If they are not breathing normally, have someone call the emergency number and bring a defibrillator.
5. If you are alone, do CPR for 1 minute first, then call.
6. Give 5 rescue breaths. For a child, pinch the nose and seal your mouth over theirs.
7. For a baby, seal your mouth over the baby's mouth and nose.
8. Blow gently and steadily for up to 1 second, until the chest rises. Let it fall, and repeat.
9. Give 30 chest compressions in the middle of the chest, pressing down one third of its depth.
10. For a child, press with one hand, or two if needed, about 5 cm (2 inches) deep.
11. For a baby, press with two fingers, or circle the chest and use both thumbs, about 4 cm (1.5 inches) deep.
12. Push fast, 100 to 120 times a minute, and let the chest come back up each time.
13. Give 2 rescue breaths, then 30 compressions. Repeat until help arrives or they breathe normally.
14. When a defibrillator arrives, switch it on and follow its spoken instructions.
15. If they start breathing normally, put them on their side and keep checking their breathing.

## Warnings

- If you can't give rescue breaths, give chest compressions only.
- If the defibrillator has no child setting, still use it. An adult shock is better than waiting.
- Do not stop CPR to check on them unless they cough, open their eyes, or breathe normally.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping the 5 rescue breaths at the start. Children's hearts usually stop because of breathing problems.
- Pressing too gently. Push firmly, one third of the depth of the chest.

## Get help

Call the emergency number as soon as possible and say the child or baby is unresponsive and not breathing. The call handler will guide you, and may ask you to use 15 compressions to 2 breaths instead.
