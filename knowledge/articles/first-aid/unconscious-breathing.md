## When to use

Use this when someone does not answer or move when you call their name and tap their shoulders, but is breathing normally.

An unconscious person's muscles relax, and the tongue can block the airway. Lying on their side keeps the airway open and lets vomit drain out.

## What you need

- Nothing but your hands

## Steps

1. Tap their shoulders and call their name. If they don't respond, shout for help.
2. Tilt their head back and lift the chin to open the airway.
3. Look, listen and feel for normal breathing, and watch whether the chest moves.
4. If they are not breathing normally, start [CPR](article:cpr) instead.
5. Kneel beside them and straighten their legs. Remove glasses and bulky things from their pockets.
6. Place the arm nearest you at a right angle to the body, elbow bent, palm up.
7. Bring their other arm across the chest. Hold the back of that hand against the cheek nearest you.
8. With your other hand, pull up their far knee so the foot is flat on the ground.
9. Keep the hand on the cheek, and pull the far leg to roll them toward you onto their side.
10. Bend the top leg at a right angle.
11. Tilt the head back and lift the chin to keep the airway open.
12. Call the emergency number, if no one has yet.
13. Stay with them and keep checking their breathing until help arrives.
14. If they lie in this position for 30 minutes, roll them onto their other side.

## Warnings

- Noisy, irregular or gasping breathing is **not** normal breathing. Treat them as not breathing and start [CPR](article:cpr).
- If you think they hurt their back or neck, still turn them onto their side so they can breathe. Keep the head and spine in a straight line.
- Do not search their pockets for small items.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving an unconscious person on their back, where the tongue can block the airway.
- Not talking to them. They may still hear you, so reassure them.

## Get help

Call the emergency number for anyone who stays unresponsive. A person who has fainted should come round quickly. If they don't, treat them as unresponsive.
