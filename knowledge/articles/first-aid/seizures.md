## When to use

Use this when someone has a seizure (fit). They may collapse, go stiff, jerk suddenly, foam at the mouth, and lose consciousness.

## What you need

- Something soft, such as a folded jacket
- A watch or phone to time the seizure

## Steps

1. Stay calm and stay with them. Note the time the seizure starts.
2. Ease them to the ground if they are falling.
3. Move them only if they are in danger, such as near a road or a hot stove.
4. Put something soft under their head.
5. Move away anything that could hurt them, and take off their glasses.
6. Loosen anything tight around their neck.
7. Let the seizure run its course. Do not hold them down.
8. When the jerking stops, turn them onto their side with the head tilted back.
9. Stay with them and talk calmly until they are fully awake.
10. Look for a medical ID card or bracelet.

## Warnings

- **Call the emergency number** if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, if it is their first seizure, or if another follows soon after.
- Also call if they are injured, have trouble breathing or waking afterward, had the seizure in water, are pregnant, or have diabetes.
- **Never put anything in their mouth**, including your fingers.
- Do not give rescue breaths during the seizure. Breathing usually starts again on its own.
- Do not give food or drink until they are fully alert.

## Common mistakes

- Holding the person down or trying to stop the movements.
- Forgetting to time the seizure.

## Get help

People with epilepsy do not always need an ambulance. If they have a care plan, follow it. If you don't know whether they have had seizures before, call the emergency number. If they stay unresponsive, see [Unconscious but breathing](article:unconscious-breathing).
