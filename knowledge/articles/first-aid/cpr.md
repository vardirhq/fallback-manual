## When to use

Use this when an adult is unresponsive and not breathing normally. Gasping, or a breath only now and then, is not normal breathing: start CPR.

This article is for adults. Children and babies need a different technique: see [CPR (children and babies)](article:cpr-children). If you have nothing else, doing CPR imperfectly is far better than doing nothing.

## What you need

- Nothing but your hands
- An AED (automated external defibrillator) if one is nearby. They are often in public places and talk you through what to do.

## Steps

1. Make sure it is safe for you to approach.
2. Shout to the person and tap their shoulders. If they do not respond, shout for help.
3. Tilt their head back gently and lift the chin. Look, listen and feel for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
4. If they are not breathing normally, call emergency services. Put the phone on speaker so your hands are free.
5. Send someone to find an AED if possible.
6. Kneel beside them. Put the heel of one hand on the center of the chest, on the lower half of the breastbone.
7. Put your other hand on top and lock your fingers together.
![Hands placed over the center of an adult's chest](image:cpr-hand-position)
8. Keep your arms straight with your shoulders directly above your hands.
![A kneeling rescuer leans over an adult with arms extended toward the chest](image:cpr-body-position)
9. Push straight down 5–6 cm (about 2 inches), then let the chest come all the way back up.
10. Push hard and fast: 100–120 times a minute, about two pushes a second.
11. If you are trained and willing, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 pushes. If not, just keep pushing without stopping.
12. As soon as an AED arrives, switch it on and do what it says. Keep pushing until it tells you to stand clear.
13. Keep going until professional help takes over, the person starts breathing normally, or you are physically unable to continue.
14. If someone else can help, swap every 2 minutes.

## Warnings

- Do not stop to check for a pulse. Keep pushing unless the person starts breathing normally.
- Ribs may crack. This is common. Keep going.
- Do not do CPR on someone who is breathing normally; put them on their side instead and watch their breathing.

## Common mistakes

- Pushing too gently, too slowly, or with bent arms.
- Not letting the chest come all the way back up between pushes.
- Long pauses, for example to look at the person or to give breaths.
- Waiting too long before starting or before calling for help.

## Get help

Always call emergency services. Anyone who needed CPR needs hospital care, even if they recover on the spot.
