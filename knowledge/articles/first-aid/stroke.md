## When to use

Use this when someone suddenly shows signs of a stroke: one side of the face droops, one arm is weak or numb, or speech is slurred or confused.

Other sudden signs are weakness or numbness down one side of the body, trouble seeing, dizziness or loss of balance, confusion, and a severe headache with no known cause. A stroke can happen at any age. Every minute counts.

## What you need

- A phone

## Steps

1. Check the face: ask them to smile. Look for one side drooping.
2. Check the arms: ask them to raise both arms. Look for one arm drifting down.
3. Check speech: ask them to repeat a simple phrase. Listen for slurred or strange words.
4. If you see any of these signs, call the emergency number right away.
5. Note the time the first symptoms started, and tell the emergency services.
6. Follow the call handler's advice while you wait for the ambulance.

## Warnings

- **Do not drive** to the hospital, and do not let someone drive the person. Call an ambulance: treatment can start on the way.
- Get help even if the symptoms stop after a few minutes. It may be a mini-stroke (TIA), which warns of a major stroke.
- The treatments that work best must start within 3 hours of the first symptoms. Do not wait.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting to see if the symptoms pass.
- Thinking the person is too young to have a stroke.
- Ignoring sudden dizziness, loss of balance or trouble seeing. These can also be signs of a stroke.

## Get help

Call the emergency number for any sign of a stroke, including signs in the last 24 hours that have now stopped. Tell the health care team what the symptoms were and when they started.
