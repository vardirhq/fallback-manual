## When to use

Use this when someone has chest pain that feels crushing, squeezing or tight, lasts more than a few minutes, or comes and goes. It may spread to the arms, neck, jaw, back or stomach, and does not ease with rest.

Other signs are shortness of breath, sweating, feeling sick or vomiting, a feeling like indigestion, feeling weak or faint, and pale, gray or bluish skin. Women more often have unusual tiredness and nausea.

## What you need

- A phone
- One 300 mg aspirin tablet, if you have one

## Steps

1. Call the emergency number right away. Say you think it is a heart attack.
2. Help them sit on the floor with knees bent and back supported, for example against a wall.
3. Give one 300 mg aspirin tablet to chew slowly, unless they are allergic to aspirin or under 16.
4. If they have their own heart medicine, such as an angina spray, help them take it.
5. Reassure them while you wait for the ambulance.
6. Keep checking that they respond and are breathing.
7. If they stop responding and are not breathing normally, start [CPR](article:cpr).

## Warnings

- **Do not drive** to the hospital. Wait for the ambulance.
- Never give aspirin to someone who is allergic to it, or to a child under 16. Do not give more than 300 mg.
- Angina pain that does not ease after 2 doses of their medicine may be a heart attack. Call the emergency number.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting to see if the pain passes.
- Thinking it is only indigestion.
- Expecting chest pain every time. Shortness of breath may come first.

## Get help

Call the emergency number at once for chest pain that feels tight or squeezing or spreads to the arms, neck or jaw, for severe trouble breathing, for pale, blue or gray lips or skin, or if the person passes out.
