## When to use

Use this for small cuts and scrapes that can be treated at home. A cut goes through the skin. A scrape only removes the top layers. For heavy bleeding, see [Severe bleeding](article:severe-bleeding).

## What you need

- Clean tap or bottled water, or sterile wipes
- Soap
- Gauze or a clean cloth that doesn't shed fluff
- A sterile dressing or adhesive bandage
- Disposable gloves, if you have them

## Steps

1. Wash and dry your hands. Put on disposable gloves if you have them.
2. If it bleeds, press on it with a clean cloth and raise the injured part until it stops.
3. Rinse the wound with clean tap or bottled water, or use sterile wipes.
4. Pat it dry with gauze or a clean cloth. Do not rub.
5. Cover the wound with gauze while you clean around it.
6. Clean the skin around it with soap and water. Wipe away from the wound, with a clean piece each time.
7. Remove the gauze and put on a sterile dressing or adhesive bandage.
8. Keep the dressing clean and dry, and change it as often as needed.

## Warnings

- If something is stuck in the wound, do not pull it out. Press on either side of it, and get medical help.
- Watch for infection: swelling, redness, pain that gets worse, or pus.
- Never give aspirin to children under 16.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving dirt in the wound.
- Rubbing the wound dry instead of patting it.

## Get help

Get medical help soon if the wound is still dirty after cleaning, came from a human or animal bite, looks infected, is longer than about 5 cm (2 inches), or comes with a fever. Also ask if you are unsure about tetanus shots. Call the emergency number if the bleeding won't stop, spurts, or the cut is very deep, or on the face or palm.
