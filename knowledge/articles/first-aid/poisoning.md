## When to use

Use this when someone may have swallowed, breathed in or touched something harmful: medicines, cleaning products, chemicals, plants or berries.

Signs include vomiting, stomach pain, burning around the mouth or throat, drowsiness, confusion, trouble breathing, seizures, or the smell of the substance on them. Signs can take hours or days to start.

## What you need

- A phone
- The packaging or container, if you can find it
- A bag or container, in case they vomit

## Steps

1. If they are unresponsive and not breathing normally, start [CPR](article:cpr).
2. If they are unconscious but breathing, put them in the [recovery position](article:unconscious-breathing).
3. If they are awake, ask what they took, how much, and when.
4. Look for empty packaging, containers, medicines, plants or berries nearby.
5. Call the emergency number, even if they seem fine. Give as much information as you can.
6. Follow the advice you are given.
7. Keep checking their breathing, and keep talking to them.
8. If they vomit, put some in a bag or container for the health workers.
9. Take the packaging, or some of the substance, to the hospital.

## Warnings

- **Do not make them vomit.** It can block the airway, and corrosive substances burn the throat again on the way up.
- Do not give anything to eat or drink unless the emergency services or a poison center tells you to. Some poison centers advise a small glass of water to rinse the throat, so ask them.
- Do not wait for signs to appear. Some poisons cause harm hours later.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting to see if symptoms develop before calling.
- Throwing away the packaging. Health workers need to know exactly what it was.

## Get help

Call the emergency number for anyone who may have swallowed, breathed in or touched something harmful, and at once if they are unconscious, have trouble breathing or have a seizure. In Norway, the Poison Information Center answers day and night on 22 59 13 00.
