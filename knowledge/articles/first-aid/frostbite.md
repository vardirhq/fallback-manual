## When to use

Use this when skin has been in cold of 0 °C (32 °F) or below, most often the fingers, toes, nose and ears. Early signs are redness or pain, then skin that is cold, numb, stinging, and red or pale.

As it gets worse, the skin turns hard, white, grayish-yellow, blue or blotchy, and may feel waxy. It can swell, form blisters, and turn black. The person may not notice, because the skin is numb.

## What you need

- A warm room or shelter
- Warm (not hot) water
- Dry blankets, clothes or a sleeping bag
- A light dressing such as gauze, if you have one

## Steps

1. Check for signs of [hypothermia](article:hypothermia): heavy shivering, confusion or drowsiness. If you see them, treat that first and get emergency help.
2. Get the person into a warm room or shelter as soon as possible.
3. Remove wet clothing, and remove rings, gloves or boots from the affected area.
4. Wrap them in dry blankets, a sleeping bag or dry clothes.
5. Put the affected area in warm, not hot, water. It should feel comfortable to an unaffected hand.
6. If you have no warm water, use body heat. For example, put frostbitten fingers in the armpits.
7. Dry the area and put on a light dressing, such as gauze.
8. Raise the affected part to reduce swelling.
9. Give them a warm drink.
10. Get medical care as soon as possible.

## Warnings

- **Do not rub or massage** the area, and never rub it with snow.
- Do not warm it with a fire, stove, radiator, heat lamp, heating pad, electric blanket or hot water bottle. Numb skin burns easily.
- Do not walk on frostbitten feet or toes unless you must. It makes the damage worse.
- Do not take a hot bath to warm up, and do not drink alcohol.
- For pain, use paracetamol (acetaminophen). Never give aspirin to children under 16.

## Common mistakes

- Not noticing frostbite because the skin is numb.
- Using hot water instead of warm water.
- Treating the frostbite and missing hypothermia, which is more serious.

## Get help

Call the emergency number if the skin is hard and frozen, swollen with no feeling, or has blisters, or if there are signs of hypothermia. Any frostbite should be checked by a health care provider.
