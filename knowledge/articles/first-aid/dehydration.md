## When to use

Use this when someone may have lost more fluid than they have taken in, for example through vomiting, diarrhea, heavy sweating or hot weather. Signs include thirst, a dry mouth, dark yellow urine, peeing less often, tiredness and dizziness.

Babies, young children and older people are at greater risk. In babies, look for fewer wet diapers, few or no tears, sunken eyes, and drowsiness.

## What you need

- Safe drinking water
- Oral rehydration solution, if you have it
- A clean cup

## Steps

1. Check for danger signs: confusion, being hard to wake, trouble breathing, or cold, pale or blotchy skin.
2. If you see any danger sign, call emergency services now.
3. If the person is awake and can swallow, give safe drinking water.
4. Start with small sips, then slowly give more.
5. After vomiting or diarrhea, give oral rehydration solution if you have it.
6. Mix the solution exactly as the packet says.
7. Keep breastfeeding or giving formula to babies, in small amounts more often than usual.
8. Keep watching for the signs listed under Get help.

## Warnings

- Confusion, being hard to wake, trouble breathing, or cold, pale or blotchy skin can mean a life-threatening emergency.
- Do not make baby formula weaker than the packet says.
- Do not give young children fruit juice or soda. They can make vomiting or diarrhea worse.
- After a lot of vomiting or diarrhea, water alone does not replace the lost salts and sugar. Use oral rehydration solution if you can.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until someone is very thirsty instead of watching how much they pee.
- Giving large drinks to someone who feels sick, instead of small sips.
- Missing fewer wet diapers or drowsiness in a baby.
- Thinking severe dehydration can always be treated at home.

## Get help

Get urgent medical advice if dizziness on standing does not go away, or the person pees much less than usual, is unusually tired, breathes quickly or has a fast heartbeat. Do the same for a baby with fewer wet diapers or few or no tears.

Call emergency services at once if the person is confused, hard to wake, struggling to breathe, or has cold, pale or blotchy skin. Severe dehydration may need fluids given in hospital through a drip.
