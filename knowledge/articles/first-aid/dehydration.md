## When to use

Use this when someone may have lost more fluid than they have taken in, especially during vomiting, diarrhoea, fever, heavy sweating or hot weather. Signs include thirst, a dry mouth, dark urine, urinating less often, tiredness and dizziness.

Babies, young children and older adults are at greater risk. In babies, look for fewer wet nappies, fewer tears, sunken eyes or unusual drowsiness.

## What you need

- Safe drinking water
- Oral rehydration solution, if available, especially after vomiting or diarrhoea
- A clean cup

## Steps

1. Check whether the person is confused, difficult to wake, struggling to breathe or has cold or unusually pale or blotchy skin.
2. Call emergency services immediately if any of those danger signs are present.
3. Offer safe drinking water in small sips if the person is alert and able to swallow.
4. Give small sips more frequently if the person feels sick or has been vomiting.
5. Use an oral rehydration solution after vomiting or diarrhoea if available, following the product's mixing instructions.
6. Continue breastfeeding or giving normal-strength formula to babies, offering feeds more often if needed.
7. Watch for worsening tiredness, dizziness, reduced urination or fewer wet nappies.

## Warnings

- Confusion, difficulty waking, breathing problems and cold or unusually pale or blotchy skin may indicate a life-threatening emergency.
- Do not delay urgent medical help for a child who is unusually drowsy, breathing quickly, passing much less urine or producing few or no tears.
- Do not dilute infant formula more than the usual instructions.
- Do not give young children fruit juice or fizzy drinks to treat vomiting or diarrhoea; these may make symptoms worse.
- Do not assume that drinking water alone replaces the salts lost through significant vomiting or diarrhoea.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting for severe thirst before paying attention to reduced urination or other warning signs.
- Offering large drinks to someone who feels sick instead of starting with small sips.
- Ignoring fewer wet nappies or unusual sleepiness in a baby.
- Treating severe dehydration as something that can always be managed at home.

## Get help

Seek urgent medical advice for persistent dizziness on standing, much less urine than usual, unusually tired or drowsy behaviour, fast breathing or heartbeat, or signs of dehydration in a baby. Seek medical care if vomiting prevents the person from keeping fluids down. Call emergency services immediately if the person is confused, difficult to wake, struggling to breathe or shows signs of shock. Severe dehydration may require treatment in hospital.
