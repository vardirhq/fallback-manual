## When to use

Use this when something is stuck in a person's throat and blocks their airway. They may clutch their throat or point to it, have a red, puffy face, and struggle to breathe, speak or cough.

This is for adults and children over 1 year old. Babies under 1 need different first aid.

## What you need

- Nothing but your hands

## Steps

1. Ask: "Are you choking?" If they can cough, speak or breathe, encourage them to keep coughing.
2. Remove anything you can clearly see in their mouth.
3. If they can't cough, breathe or make a sound, help them lean forward. Support their chest with one hand.
4. With the heel of your other hand, give up to 5 sharp blows between the shoulder blades.
5. Check their mouth after each blow, and remove anything you see.
6. If they are still choking, stand behind them and put your arms around their waist.
7. Make a fist and place it between the belly button and the bottom of the chest.
8. Grasp the fist with your other hand. Pull sharply inward and upward, up to 5 times.
9. Check their mouth after each thrust.
10. If they are still choking, call the emergency number now.
11. Repeat 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the blockage clears or help arrives.
12. If they become unresponsive, lower them gently to the ground and start [CPR](article:cpr).

## Warnings

- **Never give abdominal thrusts to a baby under 1 year old.**
- For a small child over 1, use the same steps with less force.
- Only remove objects you can clearly see in the mouth.

## Common mistakes

- Starting back blows while the person can still cough well. Encourage coughing first.
- Waiting too long to call for help when the blockage does not clear.

## Get help

Call the emergency number if back blows and abdominal thrusts have not cleared the blockage, or if the person becomes unresponsive. Keep going until help arrives.
