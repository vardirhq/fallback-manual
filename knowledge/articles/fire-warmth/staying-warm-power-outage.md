## When to use

Use this when your heating has stopped because of a power cut or fuel shortage and the home is getting cold.

## What you need

- Blankets, sleeping bags, duvets
- Warm clothing, hats, socks and gloves
- Towels, rugs or cardboard to block drafts
- Food and drinks

## Steps

1. Choose one small room to keep warm, ideally on the side of the house that gets the most sun and with few outside walls.
2. Close the doors to other rooms and block gaps under doors with towels or rugs.
3. Cover windows with blankets or curtains at night. Open curtains on sunny windows during the day.
4. Dress in several loose layers, and wear a hat and socks, including in bed.
5. Bring everyone into the warm room. Share sleeping space and use sleeping bags inside blankets.
6. Eat regular meals and drink warm drinks; your body needs fuel to make heat.
7. Keep moving a little during the day to stay warm, but avoid sweating.
8. Check on older people, babies and anyone ill, who get cold faster.

## Warnings

- Never use gas ovens, grills, camping stoves or generators to heat the inside of a home. See [Carbon monoxide poisoning](article:carbon-monoxide).
- Candles are a fire risk. Never leave them burning unattended or near fabric. Battery lights are safer.
- Watch for signs of [hypothermia](article:hypothermia): shivering, confusion, drowsiness, slurred speech.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to heat the whole house instead of one room.
- Wearing one thick layer instead of several thinner ones.
- Letting clothes get damp from sweat.

## Get help

If anyone shows signs of hypothermia, follow [Hypothermia](article:hypothermia) and get medical help. Many areas open warming centers during long outages; local radio may announce them.
