## When to use

Use this when anyone is burning fuel indoors or nearby (generators, grills, camping stoves, gas or kerosene heaters, cars in garages) and especially when people develop headaches, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, chest pain or confusion. Carbon monoxide has no smell or color.

## What you need

- Fresh air
- A battery carbon monoxide alarm, if you have one

## Steps

1. If you suspect carbon monoxide, get everyone, including pets, outside into fresh air immediately.
2. Do not go back in to open windows if people are already ill. Get out first.
3. Turn off the fuel-burning appliance only if you can do it on the way out.
4. Call emergency services if anyone has symptoms, is drowsy, or is unconscious.
5. If someone is unconscious and not breathing normally, [start CPR](article:cpr) in the fresh air.
6. Do not go back inside until the source is found and the building has aired out.

## Warnings

- Never use generators, charcoal or gas grills, or camping stoves inside a home, garage, basement, tent or covered porch, even with doors or windows open.
![US safety label: never run a generator inside a home or garage, even with doors and windows open; only outside, far from windows, doors and vents](image:generator-co-warning)
- Place generators outdoors at least 6 m (20 ft) away from doors, windows and vents.
- Never use a gas oven or stove to heat a home.
- Never run a car in a closed garage.

## Common mistakes

- Mistaking symptoms for flu or tiredness, especially when several people are affected at once.
- Thinking an open window makes indoor fuel burning safe.
- Going to sleep with a fuel heater running in a closed room.

## Get help

Anyone with symptoms needs medical assessment, even after they feel better in fresh air. Pregnant people, infants and people with heart or lung disease are at higher risk.
