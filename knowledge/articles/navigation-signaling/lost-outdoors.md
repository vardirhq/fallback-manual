## When to use

Use this as soon as you think you are lost outdoors. Panic is your greatest enemy: stay calm.

## What you need

- A map and compass, if you have them
- Water, food, warm clothes and a flashlight
- A phone, if it has battery

## Steps

1. Stop as soon as you think you are lost, and stay calm.
2. Stay where you are. Do not walk without a clear reason.
3. Think back over how you got here, and which landmarks you should see.
4. Use your map and compass to work out directions from where you stand.
5. If you are on a trail, stay on it and look for trail markers.
6. Make a plan, and move only if you are very confident of the route.
7. Stay put if it is getting dark, you are hurt, or you are close to exhaustion.
8. Rest before you get exhausted, and drink enough water.
9. Save phone battery: turn the phone off or use airplane mode until you need it.
10. Signal for help. See [Signaling for help](article:signaling-for-help).

## Warnings

- Following a stream downhill is a last resort, and can be very dangerous.
- Do not cross streams that are icy, flooded, fast-moving, or of unknown depth.
- Do not rely on a phone. There may be no signal, and it is not a light, map or survival kit.

## Common mistakes

- Panicking and walking on without a plan.
- Not telling anyone where you were going.

## Get help

Before every trip, tell someone your route, when you will be back, where you parked, and who is with you. Carry a map, compass, flashlight, extra food and water, and warm clothes.
