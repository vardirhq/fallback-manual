## When to use

Use this when a child is frightened or distressed during war or while sheltering. Children may react differently depending on their age and experience.

## What you need

- A familiar toy or quiet activity, if available

## Steps

1. Keep children close in a shelter.
2. Explain what is happening in words the child can understand.
3. Ask how the child feels.
4. Listen to their concerns and questions.
5. Answer honestly in a way the child understands.
6. Limit exposure to distressing news coverage.
7. Offer a quiet activity such as drawing or a game.
8. Keep familiar routines when possible.

## Warnings

- Do not dismiss a child’s fears.
- Do not expose children to repeated disaster news coverage.

## Common mistakes

- Ignoring questions or concerns.
- Forgetting that children can react differently to the same event.

## Get help

Contact a health professional if distress continues or affects the child’s relationships or daily activities. For immediate cover, see [air raid](article:air-raid).
