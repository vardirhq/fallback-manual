## When to use

Use this when there is a flood warning, water is rising, or your home or road is flooded. Floods kill more people than any other storm hazard, most of them in vehicles.

## What you need

- A way to get alerts, such as a phone or weather radio
- Drinking water and food for several days
- Rubber boots and gloves

## Steps

1. Listen to alerts and follow what the authorities tell you.
2. If you are told to leave, leave right away. See [Evacuation](article:evacuation).
3. If there is time and it is safe, turn off gas, power and water before you leave.
4. If you stay, move to higher ground or a higher floor.
5. Never walk, swim or drive through floodwater. Turn around.
6. Stay off bridges over fast-moving water. They can be washed away.
7. If your car is trapped in fast-moving water, stay inside. If water rises inside, climb onto the roof.
8. If you are trapped in a building, go to the highest floor. Do not climb into a closed attic.
9. Go onto the roof only if you must, and signal for help. See [Signaling for help](article:signaling-for-help).
10. Return home only when the authorities say it is safe.

## Warnings

- **15 cm (6 inches) of moving water can knock you down. 30 cm (1 foot) can sweep away a car.**
- Never drive around barriers that close a flooded road. The road may be gone.
- Floodwater can carry electricity from downed power lines, and sewage, chemicals and debris. Never touch a fallen power line.
- Do not touch electrical equipment if it is wet or you are standing in water.
- Run generators only outdoors. See [Using a generator safely](article:generator-safety).

## Common mistakes

- Driving into a flooded road. More than half of flood drownings happen in vehicles.
- Letting children play in floodwater.

## Get help

After a flood, wear rubber boots and gloves when you clean up, and wash with soap and clean water. Get medical care for a wound that touched floodwater and becomes red, swollen or painful. Ask the authorities whether the tap water is safe.
